Chromatic Enclosures — Targeting Chord Tones

Jazz guitar · Interactive tablature study

Choose a drop-3 voicing and turn its chord tones into melodic targets. For each target, play above (descending) → below (ascending) → target, choosing one or two chromatic approach notes independently on each side. This creates tension and then resolves directly onto a note of the chord.

1. Choose your harmony

Root3rd / 4th5th7th

Cmaj7 · Root position

2. Select a target chord tone

The tablature keeps each enclosure on the target's string. A lower approach fret can fall outside the original chord shape; this is intentional.

→→Target (0)

3. Practise the idea musically

Start slowly and hear the target as the point of arrival. Play each three- to five-note enclosure, then try the four targets in sequence on one continuous line of tablature. Next, place the target on a strong beat, vary the rhythm, and connect it to another chord tone.

Choose 1 for a single semitone approach note, or 2 for two successive chromatic approach notes (two semitones away, then one semitone away). Chromatic enclosures are a melodic device, not a new chord shape. The approach notes can be outside the harmony; the resolution makes their function clear. For diminished sevenths, the double-flat seventh is enharmonically equivalent to a major sixth.
