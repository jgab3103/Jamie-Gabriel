Waltz for Agnes Transcription (Marek Dykta)

Jazz transcription · Harmony · John Abercrombie

I hadn't listened to this tune from John Abercrombie's 2009 recording in years, but every time I return to it I'm reminded how distinctive his harmonic world is. Rather than treating harmony as a sequence of functional signposts, Abercrombie seems to use chords as colour — drifting through unexpected sonorities in a way that feels closer to Debussy or Messiaen than conventional jazz harmony.

This transcription reveals some wonderful harmonic moments: the movement from E♭m7(sus4) to G7(#5), the floating ambiguity of A/B, and later the luminous E♭maj7(#5). These progressions work less as traditional cadences and more as evolving textures, while melody and repetition quietly hold the form together.

For years I assumed Waltz for Agnes was composed by John Abercrombie—it simply sounds like him: airy voicings, understated lyricism and rich, spacious harmony. The piece is actually by the Polish guitarist and composer Marek Dykta, whose writing inhabits a remarkably similar harmonic landscape while remaining completely personal.

One of the most beautiful aspects of the melody is its refusal to settle neatly onto expected chord tones. Important melodic notes often arrive as expressive extensions or non-chord tones, creating friction that feels colourful rather than unresolved. It recalls Messiaen's idea that dissonance can become part of the tonal palette instead of something that demands resolution.

The broader lesson for improvisers and composers is wonderfully liberating: with careful voice leading, attention to colour, and a strong sense of form, almost any note can belong. Harmony becomes less about rules than about relationships, opening the door to the shifting, ambiguous tonal world that makes this piece so compelling.
