Follow Me Transcription (Pat Metheny)

I love playing this piece—the second track from Pat Metheny's 1997 album Imaginary Day—in solo guitar settings. Metheny has a remarkable way of writing as a guitarist rather than simply writing for guitar. His music is expressive, harmonically rich, and beautifully playable, making full use of the instrument's ability to sustain multiple tonalities through open strings and resonant voicings.

My favourite moment is the gentle harmonic shift from C major to E♭maj7♭5 and then Fmaj7♭5. The progression seems to inhabit several key centres at once, yet it feels completely natural because of its smooth voice leading and clear triadic foundations. There are no dense, extension-heavy jazz voicings—just open structures that allow each note to resonate with remarkable clarity.

The result is a fascinating kind of tonal ambiguity. You can hear echoes of G major, E minor, C major, and even E♭ major, all without the music feeling unstable. Rather than disrupting the listener through chromaticism or rapid modulation, Metheny creates movement through consonance and colour.

It's one of the defining qualities of his writing: spacious, modal harmony that feels simultaneously grounded and floating. Simple ideas, beautifully voiced, can often create more emotional depth than complexity alone.
