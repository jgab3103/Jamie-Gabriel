Teaching

I have many years of experience teaching students across a wide range of ages, backgrounds and abilities, from complete beginners to university researchers and professional musicians. I have taught subjects including harmony and analysis, composition, music history, instrumental studies and ensemble performance, as well as computer science and mathematics. I have also mentored many software developers and data scientists, and presented in settings ranging from informal workshops to academic conferences. I love learning new things and sharing them when I can. I have a PhD in Computational Musicology, as well as formal teaching qualifications for both primary and secondary schools.

I love teaching because I've had the good fortune to learn from amazing people and have spent much of my life exploring fascinating ideas with fascinating people. I enjoy sharing knowledge and helping students, whatever their starting point, develop a lifelong curiosity about the world.

On this page, you'll find many of the music teaching resources I've developed over the years. Increasingly, these are online and interactive. Despite the wealth of information available, it can still be surprisingly difficult to find high-quality, freely accessible teaching resources. Making these available to others is the main purpose of this page.

My Listening Diary

Listening is the most important part of music. It is why we are drawn to it and want to learn. It is so important to listen actively, have a sense of the history and context of what you are hearing, which will always let you find new connections and explore new music.
